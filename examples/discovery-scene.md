# Worked scene · The station is still occupied

[Wiki home](../README.md) · [Agent representation](../guides/agent-representation.md) · [Editable JSON](discovery-scene.json)

This original hypothetical scene demonstrates how vocabulary can describe one coherent moment. No footage has been generated or filmed, and no audience response has been measured.

## Plain-language direction

Mara enters an apparently abandoned transit station to recover a controller. At the workbench she notices steam rising from a cup. She stops turning her tool and listens. The camera moves gradually closer while the far doorway remains in the frame. The occupant's identity stays unknown.

## The selected vocabulary

| Dimension | Choice | What it contributes |
|---|---|---|
| Scene function | Discovery | Mara's assumption that the place is empty becomes untenable |
| Audience access | Restricted narration | The audience learns alongside Mara |
| Character action | Stop, listen, then look | The discovery becomes visible through behavior |
| Framing | Medium shot at eye level, becoming tighter | The initial work context gives way to a response |
| Camera movement | Dolly in, eight seconds | A gradual approach follows a change in attention |
| Lighting | Soft side light motivated by an overcast window | Work and expression remain readable |
| Pace | One uninterrupted eight-second shot | The pause is part of the action |
| Reference influence | Medium, camera movement only | An original written reference supplies restraint, not a copied environment |

Eight seconds is an illustrative creative choice. It is not a recommended universal duration for suspense, and the camera path has not been physically solved.

## The scene's internal progression

1. **Before:** Mara expects routine salvage. Her tool movement and attention support that expectation.
2. **Evidence:** Steam implies recent activity. The film does not need explanatory dialogue to present it.
3. **Response:** Her hand stops before her gaze moves. The delay gives the discovery a visible consequence.
4. **After:** The station is no longer understood as empty. Who is nearby remains an open question.

The next scene could lead to a friendly encounter, confrontation, false assumption, or comic interruption. The selected techniques do not determine which story must follow.

## Three ways to redirect it

**Emphasize surprise.** Keep the steam and cup out of view until Mara lifts the controller. This changes the information design; it does not require changing the character's role.

**Emphasize dramatic irony.** Show the occupant quietly watching before Mara enters. The same pause now operates with the audience knowing more than Mara.

**Emphasize routine rather than threat.** Let a familiar voice call from the doorway and have Mara continue working. Similar camera and lighting choices can support a different interpretation when context changes.

## One agent edit

Suppose the direction is “give the listening moment more time.” An agent could propose changing the camera move and shot duration from eight to twelve seconds, explaining that these are two linked decisions in this brief. It should preserve the discovery, viewpoint, and unresolved lens values unless there is a reason to discuss them.

If the request is only “slow the camera move,” the agent should notice that extending the move beyond the shot's duration creates a mismatch. This brief has no established travel distance, so it cannot yet calculate a slower physical speed. It can propose a shorter path for the same duration or a longer shot, then resolve the geometry while preserving the doorway in frame. Choosing between those options is a creative decision, not an invisible implementation detail.

## What remains undecided

The location geometry, camera path, lens, exact crop, light levels, blocking marks, and later sound cue remain unresolved. Their JSON values stay null or are listed as open questions. The `observations` array is empty because this is a brief, not a report on a finished result.
